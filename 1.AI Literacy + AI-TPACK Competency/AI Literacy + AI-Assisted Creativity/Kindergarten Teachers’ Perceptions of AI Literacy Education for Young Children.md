## Abstract Educators and researchers have increasingly recognized the importance of AI literacy. Despite the growing body of AI literacy literature, the challenges and enablers for introduc- ing AI literacy for kindergarten children is still underexplored and undertheorized in early childhood education. It is critical to understand the challenges and enablers associated with promoting AI literacy and perceptions in kindergartens needs to be further researched and evaluated. This study aims to identify teachers’ views on the importance of AI literacy in kindergartens and the challenges and enablers of promoting AI literacy in kindergartens. Data was collected from 15 teachers through individual interviews. It was found that the four main challenges of promoting AI literacy in kindergarten classrooms, including lack of school support, lack of children’s comprehensive ability, insufficient teacher knowledge of AI, and lack of curriculum guidelines. The three main enablers of promoting AI literacy in kindergarten classrooms are government support, school support, and social needs. We also found that more than half of teachers in this study considered AI literacy education is crucial for kindergarten children. This study develops an AI literacy policy framework for young children that includes three dimensions—governance, pedagogical, and operational and management. Based on the findings, suggestions for educators, curriculum developers, policymakers, and researchers are discussed.

**Keywords** AI literacy · Teachers’ perceptions · Challenges · Enablers · Kindergarten classrooms · Early childhood education · AI literacy policy framework

## Literature review

### The framework of AI literacy

AI literacy is becoming increasingly important as artificial intelligence plays a larger role in our society and throughout various industries. Recently, several studies developed an AI literacy framework in education (e.g., Kong et al., [2023;](#references) Ng et al., [2021;](#references) Wong et al., 2019). These frameworks aim to equip students with the knowledge and skills necessary to navigate an AI-driven world effectively. Kong et al. ([2023)](#references) designed an AI literacy frame- work, including cognitive, affective, and sociocultural. One study developed an AI literacy framework that includes three dimensions: AI concepts, AI applications, and AI ethics/ safety to assess students’ AI literacy (Wong et al., 2019). Ng et al. ([2021)](#references) developed an AI literacy framework that includes four dimensions: (1) understanding and knowledge of AI; (2) AI application and usage; (3) AI evaluation and creation; and (4) AI ethics. These dimensions offer a comprehensive framework that supports the development of AI literacy and enables the integration of AI into early childhood education. These three frameworks are designed to help educators and students better understand AI, its applications, and its implications.

### AI literacy in early childhood education

Su et al. ([2023)](#references) systematically reviewed K-12 teaching tools, teaching methods, and critical content to help educators to integrate artificial intelligence into the classroom effectively. This study found that the focus of AI literacy varies greatly across different educational levels. AI literacy in primary, secondary, and higher education mainly focuses on complex

However, AI literacy in early childhood education mainly focuses on basic AI concepts/knowledge (Williams et al., 2019a) and simple AI activities (e.g., Quick, Draw! and Teachable machine) (Su & Yang, [2023b;](#references) Su & Zhong, [2022;](#references) Su et al., [2023a;](#references) Vartiainen et al., 2020). Moreover, Williams (2018) designed learning activities such as drawing AI robots and sorting algorithms to help kindergarten children grasp AI concepts. It has been demon- strated that AI literacy yields numerous benefits. Therefore, developing AI literacy in kin- dergartens is a worthwhile -research area.

### Opportunities of implementing AI literacy in early childhood education

There are many benefits for kindergarten children to learn AI. To begin with, some research studies found that AI literacy programs facilitated kindergarten children’s understanding of basic AI concepts (Su et al., [2023;](#references) Williams et al., 2019a). Williams et al. (2019a) used _PopBots_ assessment to assess children’s AI literacy. Results showed that kindergarten chil- dren can understand three AI concepts after the AI curriculum: knowledge-based systems, supervised machine learning, and generative AI. Su and Yang ([2023a)](#references) used knowledge assessment to assess kindergarten children’s AI literacy. Results found that kindergarten children can understand basic AI concepts. To sum up, kindergarten children can under- stand basic AI concepts and should learn from kindergarten (Preface, [2021)](#references). In addition to enhancing children’s basic AI knowledge, two studies found that children learning AI can enhance their three inquiry skills (i.e., creative inquiry, emotional inquiry, and collaborative inquiry) (Kewalramani et al., [2021)](#references) and creativity skills (Su & Yang, [2023a)](#references). For example, regarding collaborative inquiry, “the children explored the AI toys, and shared and reflected on previous experiences, making new suggestions about how to use the toys” (Kewalramani et al., [2021,](#references) p. 662). Data was collected through video obser- vation. Results show that kindergarten children interacting with AI robots performed better in collaborative inquiry. Although some studies found that AI literacy enhanced kindergar- ten children’s AI knowledge and skills, studies discussing the challenges and enablers of AI literacy in kindergartens are absent.

However, limited empirical research focuses on the chal- lenges of implementing AI literacy in kindergartens. ## Research gaps in the literature

Most AI literacy research focused on teaching and learning AI in K-12 contexts (e.g., Lin & Van Brummelen, [2020;](#references) Williams et al., 2019). However, few studies centered on the challenges and enablers of AI literacy in kindergartens and teachers’ perceptions of AI lit- eracy in kindergartens. To fill this gap, individual interviews are used in this study to exam- ine the difficulties and facilitators of promoting AI literacy in kindergartens and understand teachers’ insights concerning the importance of AI literacy.

## Methods

### Instruments

This research aimed to investigate Hong Kong kindergarten teachers’ perceptions of AI liter- acy education in kindergartens. In this study, teacher interviews were developed to understand the challenges of promoting AI literacy in kindergarten classrooms. The researcher designed all interview questions to interview teachers regarding their perceptions of AI literacy in kin- dergarten. Subsequently, two education scholars provided feedback on these interview ques- tions, and the researcher revised the interview questions based on their suggestions. The interview questions include three parts—the first part concerns demographic infor- mation. In contrast, the second part requires teachers to answer the questions _“What are the factors that may challenges of promoting AI literacy in kindergarten classrooms”_ and _“What are the factors that enablers of promoting AI literacy in kindergarten classrooms?”_. The third part requires teachers to answer, “_Do you think AI literacy education _ _in kin-_ _dergarten is important? Why?”_. Responses from 15 teachers were collected in Septem- ber 2022. Individual interviews lasting for 30–40 min were conducted via WhatsApp. All interviews were fully transcribed for analysis.

### Participants

Teachers participating in this study from various kindergartens, allowing for the explo- ration of potential differences and similarities in teachers’ experiences and viewpoints.

Fifteen kindergarten teachers from several Hong Kong kindergartens participated in this research. The researcher personally contacted the teachers by phone to schedule teacher interviews. They were informed about the purpose of this research and the main topics of this interview. They were informed that this research was voluntary and anonymous and that they could withdraw at any time without providing a reason.

Table  [1 ](#data-analysis)describes the demographic information. Among these participants, fourteen (93.3%) teachers are female, and one (6.7%) is male. nine (60%) teachers were aged 20 -30  years old. Seven teachers (46.7%) reported having 1 to 5  years of teaching experi- ence. Fourteen (93.3%) teachers had received university degrees. Participants were asked if they had any experience learning AI. Results show that none of them had attended AI workshops.

### Data analysis

This study used interpretive description (Thorne, [2008)](#references). The important function of open- ended questions is to “give respondents opportunities to describe their experiences in more detail without the restrictions or biases inherent to fixed-choice format questions” (Chung et al., [2022,](#references) p. 4). Moreover, qualitative data collected from individual interviews can be effectively analyzed using Excel (Ose, [2016)](#references).

The challenges and enablers of promoting AI in kindergarten classrooms were coded by theme. Data analysis occurred in four steps: first, thematic analysis is used in this study, with the data being coded with the study’s research questions. Second, initial data codes were generated by the researcher and one student helper, who acted as the primary coder and the secondary coder respectively. To ensure objectivity in the coding process, the two coders coded the dataset separately before comparing. Third, potential themes in the codes were identified. The research team then discussed, reviewed, and resolved disagreements and discrepancies. At last, all articles were carefully reviewed, discussed, extracted, and coded by the coders to ensure consistency and validity of conclusions.

This study used memo notes to enhance the credibility of the findings in a qualitative study. To assess potential biases during the data analysis, the researcher documented reflec- tions and insights throughout the qualitative phase of the study.

## Results

Fifteen teachers described the challenges and enablers of promoting AI literacy in kinder- garten classrooms and articulated their thoughts on the importance of AI literacy in kinder- gartens through individual interviews.

**RQ1:** What are the main challenges of promoting AI literacy in kindergarten classrooms?

The four main challenges associated with promoting AI literacy in kindergarten class- rooms include lack of school support (nine times), lack of children’s comprehensive ability (eight times), insufficient teacher knowledge of AI, (eight times), and lack of curriculum guidelines (seven times). Other challenges include time and parents’ intentions. Parent’s intention is defined as whether or not parents want their children to learn AI literacy. ### Challenge #2: Lack of children’s comprehensive ability

If AI literacy courses are introduced to Hong Kong kindergartens, kindergarten teachers are particularly concerned about the young children’s understanding level and the curricu- lum’s appropriateness. Some teachers think that kindergarten children may be too young to grasp AI knowledge and perceive AI as distant from their daily lives. Two teachers shared their thoughts:

Due to children’s young age, they may not understand AI knowledge, which will affect the learning process. (Teacher 15)

AI may still be at distance from the lives of children, so my students may not understand AI concept or knowledge. (Teacher 9)

### Challenge #3: Insufficient teacher knowledge of AI

Hong Kong kindergarten teachers have not received any formal training in artificial intel- ligence (AI). Due to a lack of understanding and knowledge about AI, they perceive imple- menting AI courses in Hong Kong’s kindergartens to be quite challenging. Five teachers indicated insufficient knowledge of AI to promote AI literacy in kinder- gartens. All five teachers expressed that they lack the knowledge to teach AI to kindergar- teners. One teacher mentioned that:

Adequate knowledge of AI is required for an instructor  to teach these courses. (Teacher 15)

### Challenge #4: Lack of curriculum guideline

Currently, there is no specific curriculum outline for AI literacy in early childhood educa- tion, and many teachers lack a proficient background and understanding in AI. Therefore, they are uncertain about how to teach AI and design AI-related courses or activities for children. Two teachers shared their thoughts:

Our kindergarten teachers do not know how to teach AI or how to design courses. (Teacher 4)

I don’t know how to design engaging activities to attract my students to learn AI. (Teacher 8)

#### Other challenges

Other challenges of promoting AI literacy in kindergarten classrooms are time and parent’s intentions.

In schools in Hong Kong, one of the primary constraints for teachers is time. Teachers express that they lack additional time to research artificial intelligence (AI) literacy due to their regular responsibilities in teaching and various administrative duties. Two teachers remarked that:

The class schedule is so full that it is virtually impossible to allocate time for AI lit- eracy research. (Teachers 9 and 11)

How parents perceive AI literacy can significantly impact the feasibility of implement- ing such a curriculum in kindergarten. Some parents may be concerned about introducing AI literacy at a young age. They might worry that it could be too complex. It’s essential to address these concerns and educate parents about the potential benefits of AI literacy for their children’s future. One teacher shared her thoughts:

How parents perceive AI literacy may impact the feasibility of implementing this curriculum in kindergarten. Some parents may find AI literacy challenging for chil- dren aged 5-6, while others may prioritize subjects like Mathematics and English. (Teacher 13)

**RQ3:** How do Hong Kong kindergarten teachers view the importance of AI literacy in kindergartens?

Teachers responded to the question, “_Do you think AI literacy in kindergartens is impor-_ _tant? Why?_”. More than half of teachers think AI literacy education in kindergartens is important. The teacher perceptions of the importance of AI literacy in kindergarten are summarized in Table 4.

#### The Importance of AI Literacy Education in Kindergarten

Eight out of fifteen teachers stated that AI literacy in kindergartens is essential. When considering introducing AI literacy education in kindergarten, most teachers emphasize its importance. On the one hand, some teachers believe that cultivating AI literacy can enhance children’s 21st-century skills, particularly critical thinking and problem-solving abilities. On the other hand, some teachers think that with the rapid development of social technology, kindergarten children should know which artificial intelligence applications are useful in daily life. Two teachers mentioned that:

I think AI literacy in kindergarten is very important due to AI literacy can increase children’s 21st skills, namely critical thinking and problem-solving skills. (Teacher 

2. Nowadays, technology is developing rapidly, kindergarten children can understand what AI is and which AI principles apply in daily life. Therefore, AI literacy in kin- dergarten is very important. (Teacher 5)

Introducing AI literacy courses in kindergarten is seen not only as a means to benefit children in terms of technological proficiency but also as a way to enhance their collabora- tive and creativity skills. One teacher reported that:

I think AI literacy for kindergarten children is very important. Kindergarten children will enhance cooperative and creativity skills after the AI curriculum. (Teacher 11)

Incorporating AI into early education is thought to spark children’s profound interest in novel concepts during the critical stage of cognitive development. Three teachers stated that:

The early childhood period is a crucial stage in children’s cognitive development, where their curiosity towards new things is particularly strong. Introducing AI lit- eracy in kindergarten can help children to engage with and comprehend technology at an early stage. (Teachers 5, 7, 8)

#### The Unimportance of AI Literacy Education in Kindergarten

Four and three out of fifteen teachers remained neutral and disagreed that AI literacy in kindergartens is essential, respectively. The idea of introducing AI education to kindergar- ten children is a topic of controversy. For several reasons, some teachers express neutral feeling or may disagree with introducing AI literacy to the kindergarten curriculum. ## Discussion

AI literacy has gained much popularity in recent years. Many studies focused on teach- ing AI literacy from primary to higher education (e.g., Su et al., [2022)](#references). However, a lim- ited number of studies focused on teaching AI literacy in kindergartens. Little is known about the facilitation  of promoting AI literacy in kindergarten.  Despite one study explored the barriers of promoting AI literacy in education (Wang & Cheng, 2021). The study, nevertheless, was conducted in the secondary school context  (Wang & Cheng, 2021). Therefore, this is the first study probing the challenges and enablers of incorporat- ing AI literacy into kindergarten classrooms. This study also explores teachers’ perceptions of the importance of AI literacy for kindergarten teachers. 15 kindergarten teachers partici- pated in individual interviews, which collected their views on the importance of AI literacy for kindergarten, as well as the challenges and enablers of AI literacy in kindergartens in this study. Research on AI literacy in kindergarten requires input from teachers. This study develops AI literacy policy framework for young children. This study also provides future research directions as a reference for researchers. For instance, researchers may explore these areas in greater detail to raise public awareness of AI literacy. Upon review of the challenges associated with promoting AI literacy in kindergarten classrooms, results indicate four main challenges of promoting AI literacy in kindergar- ten classrooms: lack of school support, lack of children’s comprehension ability, insuffi- cient teacher knowledge of AI, and lack of curriculum guidelines. To begin, kindergarten

classrooms often lack the necessary resources, such as computers and AI robots, to teach AI concepts effectively. Without these tools, engaging young students in AI-related activi- ties is challenging. Second, kindergarten teachers may not have the knowledge or training to teach AI concepts effectively. Wang and Cheng (2021) also believe that the lack of AI knowledge among teachers is a major difficulty in introducing AI literacy to secondary schools. Addressing these challenges will require a concerted effort from various stakehold- ers. Schools should be encouraged to provide the necessary support, allocate resources, and adapt curricula to incorporate AI education. Moreover, teachers need access to pro- fessional development programs that enhance their knowledge and confidence in teach- ing AI-related topics. Collaboration between educational institutions, policymakers, and the AI community can help overcome these challenges and ensure that kindergarten stu- dents are better prepared for a technology-driven future.

Regarding the enablers of promoting AI literacy in kindergarten classrooms, this research found that the three main enablers are government support, school support, and social needs. In terms of government support, to begin with, AI literacy in kindergartens may be promoted to a greater extent if it is made mandatory. Given this, the government should consider incorporating some AI activities into kindergartens’ and the science/ ICT curriculum. Second, governments can allocate funding to kindergartens to acquire necessary resources, such as technology and educational materials. Financial support can significantly enhance the quality of AI education in kindergartens. If more govern- ment subsidies are allocated to artificial intelligence technology, the implementation of AI literacy can be further facilitated. Technological advancement has helped teachers overcome several challenges in teaching AI (Su & Yang, [2022)](#references). Third, government ini- tiatives can provide opportunities for kindergarten teachers to receive training and pro- fessional development related to AI education. This initiatives ensure educators have the knowledge and skills to teach AI concepts effectively. In terms of school support, schools can support AI literacy in kindergarten from the following aspects. To begin with, schools can provide teachers with the time and resources for professional develop- ment. Teachers need to be well-prepared to teach AI concepts, and schools can facilitate this by offering training programs and workshops. Second, when schools incorporate AI education into kindergarten, they can purchase necessary tools, such as computers and AI robots, to facilitate AI learning among children. Social needs are important in promoting AI literacy in kindergartens. With the rapid development of technology, it is becoming increasingly important for kindergarten children to understand how AI tech- nology is used in daily life, even if they do not need to understand the complexity of AI knowledge (Su et al., [2023)](#references).

We suggest hiring more support staff to assist teachers in delivering the AI curriculum. As artificial intelligence is a relatively complex course that requires in-depth expertise and practical experience. Support staff can serve as teaching assistants, assisting teachers in preparing course materials and providing additional guidance and tutoring. They can also provide technical support in the classroom to ensure children use relevant AI tools effec- tively. Hiring more support staff not only reducing teacher workloads but also enhance the quality and effectiveness of the AI courses. Therefore, hiring more support staff can help researchers implement AI literacy education in kindergartens effectively.

## Conclusion and limitations

AI literacy in K-12 contexts generally receives far more attention than in kindergartens (K1-K3). This study aims to identify the challenges and enablers of promoting AI literacy in kindergartens and analyze teachers’ views on AI literacy education  in kindergartens. Results collected from the 15 kindergarten teachers in Hong Kong pinpoint four main chal- lenges of promoting AI literacy in kindergarten classrooms: lack of school support, lack of children’s comprehensive ability, insufficient teacher knowledge of AI, and lack of curricu- lum guidelines. On the other hand, government support, school support, and social needs are found to be enablers of promoting AI literacy in kindergarten. It has also been found that more than half of teachers believe AI literacy education is crucial for young children. The challenges and enablers should be carefully considered by researchers for conduct- ing research on AI literacy. This study also develops an AI literacy policy framework for young children, including three dimensions—governance, pedagogical, and  operational and management.

This study has two limitations. To begin with, due to a relatively small sample size, this study only collected data through interviews. Other instruments, such as questionnaires and surveys, were not employed. Future research should consider incorporating a variety of research tools, such as questionnaires, surveys, and interviews, to provide a more compre- hensive understanding of the challenges and enablers associated with promoting AI liter- acy in kindergartens. Second, the study was only conducted in Hong Kong. Future research endeavors will extend invitations to schools from different regions and diverse locations to participate in this study.
